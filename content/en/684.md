I was impressed by [Seattle Wireless TV](Seattle Wireless TV) (via [Slashdot](http://slashdot.org/articles/04/06/20/0416245.shtml?tid=126&tid=137&tid=193&tid=95)). It looked like a formal media (or official media) if there is no "Help Sponsor the Show!" text or the PayPal denote icon.

The host just carries a camera and go out to catch interesting things. In the [June 2004](http://tv.seattlewireless.net/stream/june2004.asx) edition, they interviewed Drew from [WiFiMaps.com](http://www.wifimaps.com) (with Doug Luce of [Telarama](http://www.telerama.com)) and recorded a presentation of Jason Levitt of [Less Networks](http://lessnetworks.com) - "How Less Networks and Austin City Wireless Project beat T-Mobile".

The simplest camera is enough. The result can be edited by (almost) free [Movie Maker 2](http://www.microsoft.com/windowsxp/downloads/updates/moviemaker2.mspx) - to add text, subtitle and credit screens.

**Start the Same Kind of Station in Shanghai?**

It is a silly idea to start any media, especially the network media in China right now. It will be another [illegal site](http://home.wangjianshuo.com/archives/20020926_my_site_remains_illegal_in_china.htm). Someone started to use network as a media to setup their own [Internet Radio stations](http://www.google.com/search?sourceid=navclient&ie=UTF-8&oe=UTF-8&q=%E7%BD%91%E7%BB%9C%E5%B9%BF%E6%92%AD) (Google Search, Chinese), however, I worry about their future. It may not be a problem if they are small and no one notices them. But what if it goes stronger?
