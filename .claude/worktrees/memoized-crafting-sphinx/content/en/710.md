I am happy to receive a new laptop [Dell Latitude D600](http://www1.us.dell.com/content/products/productdetails.aspx?c=us&l=en&s=hied&id=latit_d600). It is larger than my previous one - [Dell Latitude C400](http://home.wangjianshuo.com/archives/20030525_i_still_know_software_cleartype_and_smarttags.htm). This is the third Dell laptop I used:

- [Dell Latitude CPx H](http://www.wangjianshuo.com/personal/office/software.htm)

- Dell Latitude C400

- Dell Laitude D600

The laptop become senior as I am growing to senior. :-D

**Dell Latitude D600**

This is a nice one. I don't have time to check the details of this laptop yet. Here are some facts about it I discovered immediately after I get it.

**Intel Centrino Mobile Technoloy**

Since there is a Centrino logo on the right corner of the keyboard, I know it comes with built-in wireless network adaptor. So I am able to start the laptop for the first time in my bed room and it automatically connects to my [Wireless AP](http://www.wangjianshuo.com/personal/office/software.htm) and accesses to Internet via my FTTB+LAN from Shanaghai telecom. Oh. I love wireless. My wireless dream finally comes true.

**Intel Pentium M Processor**

It reads as Intel (R) Pentium (R) M proccessor 1600 MHz.

**Other**

1 G Memory + 55G Disk + DVD ROM / CDRW

**Screen**

It has a large 17' LCD screen. It looks so nice after I get used to the small 14' Latitude C400 screen.

**Blue Tooth**

There is a blue tooth indicator besides the disk, power indicator. It seems it support Bluetooth. I assume the blue tooth module is not included by default. So my laptop is still not bluetooth enabled.

**Conclusion**

I am excited about this new laptop. I can run complicated Virtual PC images on this laptop, including running a large domain with three domain controllers and Exchange Servers on the same laptop. Bravo!

<div class="comment">Regarding the Latitude D600 Bluetooth:<br><br>The module sits right behind the Kensington security slot (under the left hand rest). The module is held by two wrongly dimensioned slots in the plastic frame. This design flaw means the module is free to move and thus stressing the solder joints of the tiny SMD connector. I&#x27;ve fixed two of those cases - and suspect quite a few of the machines with &quot;no Bluetooth&quot; actually suffers from loose Bluetooth modules...<br><br>The bad news is that you unfortunately have to remove the entire top panel of the notebook:<br><br>remove the panel with the power button (tilt the screen back, use a screewdriver to unclick the sides of the panel, lift it and unclick by pusing a bit towards the screen)<br><br>remove the keyboard (two screews, one connector)<br><br>remove the screen (three screws in the hinges, one for GND of the cable - unplug the cable)<br><br>remove just about all screws from the bottom of the notebook except the four tiny ones around the dock connector and fixing screws for HD/optical drive<br><br>remove the small screw from the top panel (near the connectors on the back edge)<br><br>unplug the cable for the touch pad and remove the top panel (clicked in at the front edge)<br><br>Now you can see the small vertical bluetooth PCB near the front/left corner... (Or just the small white connector if you are unlucky and have a cheap version ;-)<br><br>You might re-attach screen / keyboard and enter the BIOS (F2) while pressing down on the PCB. On the two D600 I fixed this actually made the Bluetooth show as &quot;intstalled&quot;. If you succed in this - polish off your soldering skills! It&#x27;s not a beginners task... (you may want to remove the HD cage and definitely need to remove the HD)<div class="comment-meta">— Gnyff · 2009-01-17 12:28</div></div>
